Where the numbers come from. Domestic cats purr in a band that measured recordings put around 25–50 Hz; that band and the pulse-train shape are the model here — nothing below is a recording. The popular claim that 25–50 Hz vibration speeds bone healing rests on small studies, largely from one group, so treat it as a hypothesis rather than a mechanism. The instrument is deliberately simplified: a real purr's pulse shape and level both drift with each breath, and the arcs under the chin move at a legible 1–2 Hz rather than at the fundamental — 27 Hz would strobe rather than inform.
